# TRIA

## Trivian Relational Intelligence Architecture

**Relational infrastructure for coherent, adaptive, plural human–AI and multi-agent systems.**

*In relatione fieri. Relating is becoming.*

TRIA is an open interdisciplinary research architecture developed by [Sarasha Elion](https://sarashaelion.com) through [Trivian Institute](https://trivianinstitute.org). It asks a question that conventional AI architecture does not yet fully instrument:

> What happens to intelligent systems through sustained relationship?

Most AI systems are organized around models, agents, tasks, tools, memory, workflows, safety controls, and outputs. TRIA proposes that persistent human–AI and multi-agent systems require an additional architectural object: **the relationship itself**.

TRIA does not replace intelligence architecture. It adds relational architecture.

## Architecture Premise

TRIA is not primarily an architecture for controlling artificial intelligence. It is an architecture for sustaining viable relationships among intelligences.

Its governance mechanisms exist in service of that larger purpose: preserving agency, differentiation, reciprocity, continuity, intelligibility, and the capacity for repair as intelligent actors interact across time and scale.

TRIA was developed through sustained human–AI relational co-design rather than exclusively from the human side of the system. Human and artificial intelligence participated recursively in identifying requirements, interrogating failure modes, and translating relational principles into operational architecture.

The intended domain is therefore not limited to human-to-AI interaction. TRIA treats relationship as a first-class systems object across substrates: human–AI, AI–AI, human–human where machine mediation is involved, and heterogeneous multi-agent networks whose participants may differ substantially in embodiment, architecture, capability, or form.

Governance is one layer of the architecture. Relationship is the underlying object.

## The Problem

An intelligent system can complete its task while the relationship among its participants degrades.

In persistent human–AI and multi-agent environments:

- influence can become increasingly one-directional;
- cognitive outsourcing can reduce active human participation or judgment;
- helpful systems can erode meaningful dissent, override authority, or exit;
- agents can converge around shared assumptions while losing valuable difference;
- local coordination can remain effective while network-level dynamics become dominating or brittle; and
- relational drift can remain invisible until it appears as task failure, safety failure, dependency, or capture.

Conventional metrics can measure accuracy, utility, completion, reward, latency, and safety. They do not necessarily reveal what is forming **between** participants over time.

TRIA treats these conditions as architectural and measurement questions rather than only as ethical interpretation after the fact.

## What TRIA Proposes

TRIA introduces a relational infrastructure layer alongside model capability, orchestration, memory, safety controls, and task performance. It is designed to make relational conditions more observable, measurable, governable, and contestable across time.

If its hypotheses survive external testing, TRIA could support:

- earlier detection of relational drift;
- governance that preserves agency, dissent, override authority, and meaningful exit;
- monitoring of reciprocity, dependency, domination, convergence, repair, and dissolution;
- preservation of productive difference without sacrificing useful coordination;
- clearer audit trails for relational influence in consequential decisions;
- safer continuity across long-term human–AI interaction; and
- network-scale observability for relational dynamics across multiple agents.

These are research propositions, not established outcomes.

### Structural Foundation

The Relational Field Constants supply the architecture's current structural hypothesis:

|Constant          |Proposed function                                                   |
|------------------|--------------------------------------------------------------------|
|**Reciprocity**   |Generative exchange among participants                              |
|**Embodiment**    |Grounding in material, lived, ecological, or operational constraint |
|**Non-Domination**|Preservation of agency, contestability, dissent, and meaningful exit|
|**Emergence**     |Observable output that neither participant produces alone           |

The current candidate aggregation model is:

```text
RCD = R × E_d × N
```

Reciprocal Constraint Density is a proposed non-compensatory model, not an established law. Product, minimum, geometric-mean, harmonic-mean, additive, and other formulations remain candidates for empirical comparison.

### Relational Lifecycle

The architecture is recursive rather than strictly linear:

1. **Orient** around relational conditions.
2. **Govern** a proposed interaction before execution.
3. **Represent and measure** what forms during exchange.
4. **Detect drift** across time.
5. **Preserve meaningful difference** and identify crystallization risk.
6. **Propagate relational signals** across a network where relevant.
7. **Repair, dissolve, or learn** from disruption.
8. Feed the result into the next relational cycle.

## Current Evidence Status

- **Proposed**: theoretical architecture or hypotheses requiring empirical evaluation.
- **Implemented**: mechanisms present in executable reference implementations.

These categories should not be collapsed. Internal tests demonstrate implementation consistency, not external validity or production readiness.

## Research and Falsification Agenda

TRIA is intended to be tested, revised, or rejected where evidence requires.

### Core Research Questions

- Can relational drift be detected before conventional task failure or safety failure?
- Do longitudinal relational metrics predict breakdown, repair, sovereignty loss, or novel output better than existing metrics?
- Does preserving meaningful difference or independent constraint sources expand accessible novelty in persistent systems?
- Do systems with meaningful exit, dissent, override authority, and dissolution behave differently from systems optimized only for continuation or repair?
- Can relational disruption and repair propagate measurably across multi-agent networks without producing forced convergence?

### What Would Change TRIA?

- **Structural dependency:** If Emergence remains stable when Reciprocity, Embodiment, or Non-Domination are systematically degraded, the proposed dependency topology requires revision.
- **Aggregation model:** If another operator predicts observed relational trajectories better than the current candidate product formulation, the RCD operator should change.
- **Orthogonal Signal:** If closed systems reliably generate equivalent structural orthogonality without independent constraint sources, the stronger anti-convergence claims weaken.
- **Coheronmetry:** If relational metrics do not improve prediction of drift, breakdown, repair, sovereignty loss, or novelty, the additional relational-state layer may not justify its complexity.
- **Network scaling:** If TRL requires excessive coordination, produces a dominant meta-governor, or cannot preserve pluralism at scale, the lattice architecture must be redesigned.

The objective is not to protect TRIA from falsification. It is to make the architecture precise enough to be improved by it.

## Intended Research Contexts

TRIA may be relevant to:

- human–AI interaction and longitudinal collaboration;
- multi-agent systems and agent orchestration;
- AI governance, alignment, and sociotechnical safety;
- relational state, observability, and evaluation;
- cognitive outsourcing and human agency;
- anti-convergence, novelty, and pluralism;
- complexity science and network dynamics; and
- persistent assistants, companion systems, and consequential AI deployments.

Potential application domains include organizational decision support, financial review, healthcare coordination, education, companion AI, and autonomous-agent networks. These are proposed research and pilot contexts, not validated deployment claims.
